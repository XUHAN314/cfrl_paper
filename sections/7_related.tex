\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Diffusion and flow language models.}
Discrete diffusion language models, including SEDD, MDLM, and LLaDA \citep{lou2023sedd,sahoo2024mdlm,nie2025llada}, generate through discrete or masked-token trajectories. Continuous-state language generation instead diffuses token embeddings, beginning with Diffusion-LM \citep{li2022diffusionlm}. Although earlier continuous models lagged behind discrete diffusion, ELF \citep{hu2026elf} closes this gap and outperforms discrete counterparts by using flow matching \citep{lipman2022flow,liu2022rectified,albergo2023stochastic} in a continuous embedding space and decoding tokens only at the trajectory endpoint.

\paragraph{RL for denoising generative models.}
RL for image diffusion commonly casts denoising as a sequential decision process and applies score-function policy gradients, as in DDPO and DPOK \citep{black2023ddpo,fan2023dpok}. Flow-GRPO, DanceGRPO, and MixGRPO adapt this approach to flow-matching models \citep{liu2025flowgrpo,xue2025dancegrpo,li2025mixgrpo}, while FPO uses the flow-matching objective as a likelihood surrogate within PPO \citep{mcallister2025fpo}. RL for discrete diffusion language models relies on score-function updates using one-step likelihood approximations, variational objectives, or actions at intermediate denoising states \citep{zhao2025d1,zhu2025llada15,shao2026papo}. 

\paragraph{RL for autoregressive language models.}
RL post-training has enabled autoregressive language models, including OpenAI-o1 \citep{openai2024o1} and DeepSeek-R1 \citep{deepseek2025r1}, to become strong reasoners. These methods optimize categorical token actions with score-function methods such as PPO and GRPO \citep{shao2024deepseekmath,schulman2017ppo}, with recent variants modifying clipping and sampling \citep{yu2025dapo,zheng2025gspo}. 

% \paragraph{Pathwise optimization and actor--critic methods.}
% Reward back-propagation for image diffusion differentiates a fixed reward model through one denoising step, a truncated trajectory, or the full sampler \citep{xu2023imagereward,clark2023draft,prabhudesai2023alignprop,domingoenrich2024adjoint}. Our setting differs because a program verifier is non-differentiable: we learn an intermediate-state value from verifier outcomes and differentiate that value through the sampler. This follows the stochastic value-gradient principle \citep{heess2015svg}, in which a learned value is differentiated through a reparameterised policy and transition model, and is related to actor--critic methods for continuous control \citep{lillicrap2015ddpg,haarnoja2018sac}. Here the transition model is the flow sampler itself, and the critic is updated jointly with the denoiser policy.

Our amortisation loop is related to verifier-guided self-training methods such as STaR, RFT, and ReST-EM \citep{zelikman2022star,yuan2023rft,singh2023restem,huang2022selfimprove}, as well as expert iteration \citep{anthony2017exit}: the policy generates candidate solutions, verified samples are retained, and the model is fine-tuned on them. In our framework, this final stage transfers improvements learned under the RL sampler to cheaper sampling procedures.
