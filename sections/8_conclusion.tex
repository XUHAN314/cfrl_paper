\section{Conclusion}
A flow language model's action is a path of noise coordinates, not a sequence of decisions. We introduced \method{} and studied three variants. \methodGRPO{} works, but averages every reward bit over five orders of magnitude more nuisance than the score-function estimator was designed for; the price is modest gains where there is most to learn. \methodSVGScalar{} exploits the differentiable chain and improves the policy, but its gain flattens after a few hundred iterations. \methodSVGTwoD{}, our principal contribution, keeps the update improving through a value over flow steps and token positions that pools to the actor's objective and is trained jointly on the policy's own rollouts. It leads \methodGRPO{} on every task, by about a point on Sudoku and by seven to eleven points elsewhere, and its verified samples then amortise into cheap chains. \emph{Limitations:} one 105M architecture, four synthetic tasks with exact verifiers, a held-out stopping point for \methodSVGTwoD{}, and three \methodGRPO{} seeds trained under the cheap chain.
