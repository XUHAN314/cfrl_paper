\section{Conclusion}
A flow language model's action is a path of noise coordinates, not a sequence of decisions, and we studied its post-training at three levels. The ported score-function estimator (GRPO) works, but averages every reward bit over five orders of magnitude more nuisance than it was designed for; the price is modest gains where there is most to learn and sensitivity to the seed. The pathwise estimator that the differentiable chain admits improves the policy with a scalar critic, but the improvement stops early: a pooled value is a target the actor can raise faster than the head is refit. The two-dimensional critic, our contribution, keeps the update improving: a value over flow steps and token positions that pools to the actor's objective and is trained jointly on the policy's own rollouts under the deployment sampler; handing it to a policy trained with the scalar critic recovers most of the gap, and handing the scalar critic to a policy trained with it stops the gain. It leads the score-function baseline on every task, by about a point on Sudoku and by seven to eleven points elsewhere, and its verified samples then amortise into cheap samplers. \emph{Limitations:} one 105M architecture, four synthetic tasks with exact verifiers, a held-out stopping point for \jpc{}, and GRPO trained under the cheap sampler.
