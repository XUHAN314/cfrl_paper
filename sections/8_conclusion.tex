\section{Conclusion and Limitations}
We propose \method{}, RL post-training for continuous flow language models in three variants and an amortisation loop. \methodGRPO{} is the score-function estimator on the chain's exact density; \methodSVGScalar{} and \methodSVGTwoD{} are stochastic value gradients with one value per state, or per state and token position. \methodGRPO{} gains little: one reward bit is spread over the chain's noise coordinates, and its gradient agrees with itself no better than a zero-mean control, whereas GPT-2's gradient, with one score term per token decision, does. Differentiating the chain through a critic removes the noise factor. The critic must read the denoiser's estimate through a copy of the pre-trained denoiser and be refit after every actor step, or it is exploited. With one value per position the policy rises faster and further than with a scalar value, and exchanging the critics mid-training shows that the critic in charge decides. The loop carries the gain into a cheap chain.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Our results cover one 105M flow model and four synthetic reasoning tasks with exact verifiers; larger models, natural-language tasks and learned rewards remain untested. The RL gain is tied to the sampler used in training, and carrying it to cheaper samplers requires the amortization loop and a pool of unlabelled problems. Finally, the autoregressive reference still leads on two of the four tasks, so the advantage of a flow model with RL is task-dependent rather than uniform.
