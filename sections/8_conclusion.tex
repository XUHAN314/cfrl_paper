\section{Conclusion}
A flow language model's action is a path of noise coordinates, not a sequence of decisions, and we studied its post-training at three levels. The ported score-function estimator (GRPO) works, but averages every reward bit over five orders of magnitude more nuisance than it was designed for; the price is modest gains where there is most to learn. The pathwise estimator that the differentiable chain admits improves the policy with a scalar critic, but the improvement flattens after a few hundred iterations, and the exchange experiment localises the difference to the critic in charge while the policy moves; what we can bound points to the 2D head's per-position slope under the refit, not to a better value at any fixed policy. The 2D critic, our contribution, keeps the update improving: a value over flow steps and token positions that pools to the actor's objective and is trained jointly on the policy's own rollouts under the deployment chain; handing it to a policy trained with the scalar critic recovers most of the gap, and handing the scalar critic to a policy trained with it stops the gain. It leads the score-function baseline on every task, by about a point on Sudoku and by seven to eleven points elsewhere, and its verified samples then amortise into cheap chains. \emph{Limitations:} one 105M architecture, four synthetic tasks with exact verifiers, a held-out stopping point for \jpc{}, and GRPO's three seeds trained under the cheap chain.
