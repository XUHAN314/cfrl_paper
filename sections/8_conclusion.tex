\section{Conclusion}
A flow language model's action is a path of noise coordinates, not a sequence of decisions, and we studied its post-training at three levels. The ported score-function estimator (GRPO) works, but averages every reward bit over five orders of magnitude more nuisance than it was designed for; the price is modest gains where there is most to learn and sensitivity to the seed. The pathwise estimator that the differentiable chain admits can reach high single-run accuracies with a scalar critic but is fragile at the learning rate it needs. The two-dimensional critic, our contribution, makes the pathwise recipe replicate: a value over flow steps and token positions that pools to the actor's objective, trained jointly on the policy's own rollouts under the deployment sampler. It matches the score-function baseline to within a point where the base already solves most problems and leads clearly where it does not, and its verified samples then amortise into cheap samplers. \emph{Limitations:} one 105M architecture, four synthetic tasks with exact verifiers, a held-out stopping point for \jpc{}, and GRPO trained under the cheap sampler.
