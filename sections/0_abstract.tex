\begin{abstract}
Reinforcement learning (RL) has successfully enhanced reasoning in autoregressive and discrete diffusion language models through token-level and sequence-level denoising actions, respectively. The recently introduced Embedding Language Flow (ELF) presents a different setting: under stochastic sampling, each flow step injects noise while denoising an entire sequence of continuous token embeddings, without sampling discrete tokens or assigning them probabilities. The model continuously refines the entire sequence and decodes all token positions together only at the end of the trajectory. This continuous-space refinement followed by joint discrete decoding creates a distinct action space and credit-assignment problem, leaving effective RL post-training for continuous flow language models unresolved. We investigate this problem in three stages. First, we adapt GRPO by treating each denoising transition as an action. Second, we introduce Joint Pathwise Critic (JPC), a pathwise actor-critic framework with a scalar state critic. Finally, we introduce a novel \hl{dual-axis (2D) critic} indexed by both axes: the \emph{flow step}, which captures when a state is evaluated, and the \emph{token position}, which captures where the outcome is determined. The 2D critic is trained jointly with the policy from verifier outcomes under the deployment sampler. An amortisation stage then distils verified policy samples back into the model, transferring the improvements to cheaper samplers. Across four verifiable reasoning tasks, our JPC-2D framework provides, to our knowledge, the first reliable RL-driven improvement of a continuous flow language model, while its score-function and scalar-critic variants yield smaller or less consistent gains.
\end{abstract}
