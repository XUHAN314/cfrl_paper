\begin{abstract}
Reinforcement learning (RL) has successfully enhanced reasoning in autoregressive and discrete diffusion language models through token-level and sequence-level denoising actions, respectively. Recently introduced Embedding Language Flow presents a different setting: its stochastic sampler jointly denoises continuous token embeddings while reinjecting noise at each flow step, without sampling discrete tokens or assigning them probabilities during denoising. All positions are decoded only at the trajectory endpoint. This separation between continuous generation and discrete readout creates a distinct action space and credit-assignment problem, leaving effective RL post-training unresolved. We introduce \method{}, a post-training framework for continuous flow language models that supports three policy-optimization variants: \methodGRPO{}, a score-function GRPO adapted to continuous flow generation; \methodSVGScalar{}, an stochastic value gradients (SVG) based actor-critic with a scalar state critic; and \methodSVGTwoD{}, which introduces a novel \hl{2D critic} indexed by the \emph{flow step} (when a state is evaluated) and the \emph{token position} (where the decision is represented). An amortisation loop then fine-tunes the model on its own verified samples, raising the accuracy of cheaper samplers. Across four reasoning tasks, \method{} provides, to our knowledge, the first reliable RL improvement of a continuous flow language model.

\end{abstract}
