\begin{abstract}
Reinforcement learning (RL) enables reasoning in autoregressive models by treating each token as an action with an exact conditional probability, allowing advantages to reinforce or suppress sampled tokens. RL methods for discrete diffusion language models instead treat each denoising step as an action and commonly use likelihood surrogates with sequence-level advantages shared across token updates. Continuous flow language models have no discrete token actions: they denoise an entire sequence of token embeddings and decode tokens only at the end. This creates a distinct action space and credit-assignment problem, leaving effective RL post-training for continuous flow language models unresolved. To fill this gap, we investigate three approaches. First, we adapt GRPO by treating each denoising transition as an action. Second, we introduce Joint Pathwise Critic (JPC), a pathwise actor-critic framework with a scalar state critic. Finally, we introduce a novel \hl{dual-axis (2D) critic} indexed by both axes:  the \emph{flow step}, which captures when a state is evaluated, and the \emph{token position}, which captures where the outcome is determined. The 2D critic is trained jointly with the policy from verifier outcomes under the deployment sampler. An amortisation stage then  distils verified policy samples back into the model, transferring the improvements to cheaper samplers. Across four verifiable reasoning tasks, our dual-axis JPC reliably improves a continuous flow language model, while its score-function and scalar-critic variants yield smaller or less consistent gains.
\end{abstract}
