\begin{abstract}
Reinforcement learning (RL) has successfully enhanced reasoning in autoregressive and discrete diffusion language models through token-level and sequence-level denoising actions, respectively. The recently introduced Embedding Language Flow presents a different setting: its stochastic sampler jointly denoises continuous token embeddings while reinjecting noise at each flow step, without sampling discrete tokens or assigning them probabilities during denoising. All positions are decoded only at the trajectory endpoint. This separation between continuous generation and discrete readout creates a distinct action space and credit-assignment problem, leaving effective RL post-training unresolved. We investigate this problem in three stages. First, we study score-function GRPO, systematically varying credit across flow steps and token positions. Second, we introduce Joint Pathwise Critic (JPC), a pathwise actor--critic framework that jointly trains the policy and a scalar state critic on verifier-labeled rollouts. Finally, we extend JPC with a novel \hl{dual-axis (2D) critic} indexed by both axes: the \emph{flow step}, which captures when a state is evaluated, and the \emph{token position}, which captures where the outcome is determined. An amortisation stage then distils verified policy samples back into the model, transferring the gains to cheaper samplers. Across four verifiable reasoning tasks, our JPC-2D framework provides, to our knowledge, the first reliable RL improvement of a continuous flow language model.

\end{abstract}
