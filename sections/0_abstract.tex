\begin{abstract}
Reinforcement learning turns autoregressive language models into reasoners by treating each generated token as a discrete action with an explicit probability and pushing the probabilities of rewarded tokens up. Continuous flow language models have no such actions: they denoise a whole sequence of token embeddings from Gaussian noise over a number of flow steps and read out tokens only at the end, so a sample is a differentiable function of the noise rather than a sequence of decisions. This paper asks how such a model should be post-trained with a verifiable reward, and answers it at three levels. First, the autoregressive recipe can be ported: the likelihood-ratio gradient of a denoising step is well defined, and GRPO improves every task we study, but its reward bit is spread over the noise coordinates of the whole trajectory, so its gains are small where there is most to learn and vary across seeds. Second, the differentiable chain admits the pathwise estimator instead: a critic that scores a noisy intermediate state, trained jointly with the policy from verifier outcomes, back-propagated through the denoiser at one random flow step; with a scalar critic this can produce large single-run gains but is fragile at the learning rate it needs. Third, our contribution: a \hl{two-dimensional critic}, a learned value over the two axes along which credit lives in a flow model, the \emph{flow step} (when, along the trajectory, a state is evaluated) and the \emph{token position} (where, along the sequence, the outcome is decided), which scores every position of the state and pools to the value the policy ascends. It is the level that replicates across seeds and tasks, matches the score-function baseline to within a point where the base already solves most problems, and leads clearly where it does not, including a set-split Game24 that tests generalisation to unseen number sets. An amortisation stage then distils the policy's verified samples back into the model so that cheaper samplers inherit the gains, and a masked-diffusion baseline (d1 on LLaDA-8B) run on the same data completes the picture.
\end{abstract}
