\begin{abstract}
Reinforcement learning (RL) enables reasoning in autoregressive models by treating each token as an action with an exact conditional probability, allowing advantages to reinforce or suppress sampled tokens. Discrete diffusion language models instead treat each denoising step as an action and commonly use likelihood surrogates with sequence-level advantages shared across token updates. Continuous flow language models have no discrete token actions: they denoise the entire sequence of token embeddings and decode tokens only at the end. This changes the action space and credit-assignment problem, leaving effective RL post-training for continuous flow language models unresolved. To fill this gap, we investigate the problem in three stages: we first adapt GRPO by treating each denoising transition as an action; we then exploit differentiability through a pathwise estimator that back-propagates a scalar critic value; and finally we introduce a novel \hl{dual-axis (2D) critic} over two axes:  the \emph{flow step} (when, along the denoising trajectory, a state is evaluated) and the \emph{token position} (where, along the sequence, the outcome is decided). The 2D critic is trained jointly with the policy from verifier outcomes under the deployment sampler, and an amortisation stage distils the verified samples from the policy  back into the model for improved performance with cheaper samplers. We demonstrate that our framework reliably improves a continuous flow language model across four verifiable reasoning tasks, while ported score-function methods and the scaler critic method yield smaller or less consistent gains.
\end{abstract}
